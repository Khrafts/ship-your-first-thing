# When to ask a person: the conditions for stopping

By the end of this lesson you can recognise the three conditions that mean steering has stopped working, and hand the problem to a person in three parts: what you saw, what you asked for, what came back, with nothing pasted.

> **Last verified:** 2026-08-20. If your agent behaves differently from what this lesson describes, open the lesson chat on the course site ("Ask about this lesson") and tell it what you see. Change log: [`WHAT-CHANGED.md`](../../WHAT-CHANGED.md).

Nothing runs in this lesson and nothing touches your app.

## The three conditions

None of them is about how hard the problem feels. These are things you can count.

**You have restated the same thing three times.** Two or three rounds is normal, and Module 3 gave you the fresh-conversation move for when tightening stops helping. This is one step past that: you restated, started fresh, restated again, and got recognisably the same answer. It is no longer a wording problem.

**"Done" keeps arriving and the check keeps failing.** The results are there, and then you open the app and the thing still does not do what you meant. Once is ordinary. Three times means you and your agent disagree about what the words mean, and neither of you can see where.

**What is at stake is money, other people's information, or something you cannot take back.** This one applies before you have tried anything. You already ask before any single step that cannot be undone. This is the wider case, where the whole situation carries that weight and the person who pays for a wrong call is not you.

"I do not understand what it is doing" is not a condition. You have never understood what it is doing; that has been the arrangement since Module 2. Feeling out of your depth is not a condition. Being unable to move is.

## What to bring

The three things any helper will ask for:

- **What you saw**: the actual behaviour, on the actual screen.
- **What you asked for**: in the words you used.
- **What came back**: what happened instead, and how many times.

**Do not bring code, error text, or anything else your agent produced.** You have not read it and cannot vouch for it. And what you paste decides which question you have asked: a description asks *can you help me with this problem*; a screenful of code asks *can you review this*, a much larger favour you cannot take part in.

## Who to ask

Somebody who builds software. They do not need to have seen the kind of app you built; anyone who has been stuck in a loop with a tool will recognise all three conditions.

The people already around the project. The person who reported the fault can tell you what they did. Whoever else uses it can tell you whether they see it too. On the first two conditions that is often the better help.

And the honest third answer: you may not have anybody yet. Notice that on a calm afternoon. Finding the person is a job for a day when nothing is broken.

## Stopping is a move

The fourth restatement costs the hour, the next hour, and some of your appetite for the whole project. What keeps people going is rarely a belief that the next message will work; it is the feeling that stopping is an admission. People who do this for a living ask each other things all day. Your unusual position is not that you get stuck. It is that you have fewer people to ask, and that is fixable in advance.

The opposite mistake is real too. Stopping at the first difficulty means never finding out that the second attempt usually works. Three restatements, three failed checks, or something that cannot be taken back. Not a mood.

## Your turn

Nothing runs. About fifteen minutes.

1. Pick the longest you have been stuck anywhere in this course, or on your own project since.
2. From memory, write the handover in three short parts: what you saw, what you asked for, what came back. Write it for somebody who has never opened your app.
3. Count the rounds: how many times you said the same thing in different words before something moved. Write the number down.
4. Read it back and cut anything that is not one of the three parts, especially anything copied from your agent's replies.
5. Name your person: somebody who builds things, somebody using your app, or a place you would go looking. If the honest answer is that you do not have one, write that down. It is the most useful line on the page.
6. Keep it with what you wrote down in Lessons 1 to 3.
